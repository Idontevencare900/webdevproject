Types of Cookies and Privacy

Types of Cookies

Cookies can be grouped by how long they last, and by who sets them.

Type Description Example
Session cookie Temporary. Deleted when you close the browser. Keeping items in a shopping cart while you browse.
Persistent cookie Stays on your device until it expires or you delete it. Remembering your login for weeks.
First-party cookie Set by the website you are visiting. A store remembering your preferences.
Third-party cookie Set by a different website, such as an advertiser, whose content appears on the page. An ad network recognizing you on many different sites.

Cookies and Your Privacy

First-party cookies pose no danger and actually make web surfing easier. However, third-party cookies are more of a threat when it comes to privacy issues. Since the same advertiser places cookies across many sites, he/she can create a profile of the websites visited and the items of interest.

Privacy Laws

Some governments have passed laws that affect how websites use cookies.

  • GDPR (European Union): requires websites to get a visitor's consent before using non-essential cookies. This is why you see cookie banners.
  • CCPA (California): gives residents the right to know what personal data is collected and to opt out of its sale or sharing.

How Browsers Handle Third-Party Cookies

Safari, Firefox, and Brave block third-party cookies by default. Google Chrome does not block them by default. However, users can manage third-party cookies in Chrome settings.

What You Can Do

  1. Open your browser's privacy settings and review the cookie options.
  2. Choose to block third-party cookies.
  3. Delete your cookies from time to time.
  4. Read cookie banners and decline non-essential cookies when you can.
